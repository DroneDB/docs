## When to use it

Use the measure tools to get a length, an area or the position of a point from an orthophoto or
from any other data on the map. Anyone who can open the map can measure. Users with write access
save the measurements in the dataset, so the next visitor sees them too.

## Steps

<Steps>

1. Open an orthophoto on the map, or open the **Map** tab of a dataset. The measure toolbar is on
   the map. Saved measurements of the file show automatically.

   <Screenshot id="measure/map--toolbar" />

2. Click **Measure Length**. Click the points of the line on the map. Double-click to finish.
   Press `Esc` to cancel.

3. Click **Measure Area**. Click the corners of the area. Double-click to finish. The area shows
   in the middle of the polygon.

   <Screenshot id="measure/map--area" />

4. Double-click a measurement to open its properties. Give it a name and a description, change
   its colours, its line width and its fill opacity, or put it in a group. Click **Save**.

   <Screenshot id="measure/map--properties" />

5. Click **Measurement List** to see all the measurements. Click **New Group** to make a group
   with a name and a colour. Use **Move to group** on a row to put a measurement in a group. The
   eye button of a group hides or shows its measurements.

   <Screenshot id="measure/map--list" />

6. Click **Save Measurements** to save them in the dataset.

7. To use the measurements in another program, click **Export Measurements as GeoJSON** or
   **Export Measurements as CSV**. The CSV opens in Excel or any spreadsheet.

   <Screenshot id="measure/map--export" />

</Steps>

## Options

The first row of the toolbar has the drawing tools. The second row shows when there are
measurements.

| Button | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Point Annotation** | Puts a point with a description on the map. See [Point annotations](/hub-guide/measure/annotations.mdx). |
| **Measure Length** | Draws a line through two or more points and shows its length. |
| **Measure Area** | Draws a polygon and shows its area. |
| **Erase Measurement** | Deletes the measurement you click. |
| **Edit/Move Measurements** | Drag a point to move it. `Shift`+drag moves the whole measurement. Double-click opens the properties. `Ctrl+Z` undoes. `Esc` exits. |
| **Stop Measuring** | Cancels the current tool (`Esc`). |
| **Measurement List** | Opens the list of measurements and groups. |
| **Save Measurements** | Makes the saved file the same as the map. With nothing left on the map, it removes the saved measurements. |
| **Clear All Measurements** | Removes all measurements from the map. The saved file does not change until you save again. |
| **Export Measurements as GeoJSON** | Downloads the measurements, with their groups, as a GeoJSON file. |
| **Export Measurements as CSV** | Downloads one row per measurement as a CSV table. See [CSV columns](#csv-columns). |
| **Delete Saved Measurements** | Deletes the saved measurements from the dataset. |

### How lengths and areas are measured

Hub measures lengths and areas on the WGS84 ellipsoid, not on a sphere and not on a flat map. A
length is the sum of the geodesic distances between the points. An area is the area of the
polygon on the ellipsoid. The numbers are the same as the ones that `ddb` gives, and the area of
a polygon is the same as its 2D area in the [stockpile](/hub-guide/analyse/stockpile.mdx) panel.
The stockpile panel counts pixels, so its area can differ from the map's by the pixels along the
edge of the polygon. The result does not depend on the latitude: a 100 m line east to west
measures 100 m at 0° and at 70°.

### Units

| Value | Metric | Imperial |
|---|---|---|
| Length | m up to 100 m, then km | ft |
| Area | m² below 1 ha, ha below 100 km², then km² | ft² below 1 acre, then acres |

To change the units, click **Map Settings** on the map and select **Measurement Units**:
**Metric** or **Imperial**. If there are measurements on the map, the **Change Units** dialog asks
first. Click **Convert** to show all measurements in the new units. If you can write the dataset,
Hub then saves them in the new units; if you can only read it, nothing is saved. The units
are the same setting as the **Units** tab of the [preferences](/docs/hub-guide/account/preferences),
so they follow your account. Saved lengths and areas show in your current units when they load.
The label of a [stockpile](/hub-guide/analyse/stockpile.mdx) on the map and its row in the
**Measurement List** follow the units too, after **Convert** and after you reload the page: with
**Imperial** they show yd³ and ST/yd³. The saved values stay metric.

### CSV columns

| Column | Content |
|---|---|
| `group` | The name of the group of the measurement, or empty |
| `name`, `description` | The name and the description from the properties |
| `type` | The kind of measurement: `length`, `area`, `point`, `stockpile` … |
| `value`, `unit` | The length or the area in the units the map shows. A stockpile has its net volume in m³ or yd³; a point has no value |
| `longitude`, `latitude` | The position of a point, in WGS84 |
| `wkt` | The shape of the measurement as WKT, in WGS84 |

Other properties of the measurements follow in more columns.

### Coordinates of a point

The label of a point shows its position in WGS84 in two formats: degrees, minutes and seconds,
and decimal degrees (6 decimals). The properties of a point have a button that copies the
coordinates as `latitude, longitude`.

## Limits & requirements

- **Save Measurements** shows only for users with write access to the dataset.
  **Delete Saved Measurements** shows only for users who can delete in the dataset.
- The measurements belong to a file. Hub saves them next to it, with `_measurements.geojson` in
  place of the extension: `odm_orthophoto.tif` saves `odm_orthophoto_measurements.geojson`. On
  the **Map** tab, they belong to the first orthophoto, raster or point cloud on show; with none,
  to a `measurements.geojson` file in the folder.
- The map and the [point cloud viewer](/hub-guide/measure/3d.mdx) use the same file for the same point cloud.
  Each view saves and deletes only its own measurements, and keeps the measurements and groups
  of the other view in the file.
- Hub writes the file in format 2.0. A file in an older format opens as it is and is upgraded the
  next time you save. Opening a file never changes it.
- When the map changes to another file, unsaved changes are lost. Hub shows a warning.
