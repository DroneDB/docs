## When to use it

Use the stockpile tool to measure how much material a pile holds: gravel, sand, soil, coal.
Hub computes the volume from a surface model (DSM) of the pile. Select the material, and Hub
also gives the weight and the cost of the pile.

## Steps

<Steps>

1. Open the DSM on the map. Click **Stockpile Volume** in the toolbar at the top right.

   <Screenshot id="analyse/stockpile--panel" />

2. Optional: choose the base in **Base Plane Method**. The information button next to it explains
   each method. Until you choose one, a clicked pile uses **Best-fit plane** and a drawn polygon
   uses **Lowest perimeter**. The base you choose is the base of the pile you work on: Hub measures
   that pile again with it, and no other pile changes. With no pile of the map chosen, it is the base
   of the piles you find or draw next, and of the list of **Find all piles**.

3. Click **Click map**: the button reads **Click map…** while it waits for your click. Then click on the pile. Hub outlines the pile down to its foot and
   computes its volume against the ground around it, so detection works on a slope too. Check the
   outline: when it does not reach the foot of the pile, the **Confidence** is below 50 %. Then
   change the **Detection Sensitivity** or the **Search Radius**, or click **Draw** and draw a
   polygon around the pile. Double-click, right-click or press `Enter` to finish; `Esc` cancels. On a slope, choose **Best-fit plane** for a
   drawn polygon.

   <Screenshot id="analyse/stockpile--detect" />

   <Screenshot id="analyse/stockpile--draw" />

   On a phone or in a small map, the panel folds its settings behind **Settings** while a result shows, so
   the result stays in view. Click **Settings** to open them.

   To find every pile of the raster at once, click **Find all piles** (it reads **Finding piles…** while it
   works). It scans the whole raster with the **Detection Sensitivity** above. Hub draws every pile it found
   on the map, with the number of its row, and lists them with their area, their **Volume** and their
   confidence. The list is **Piles found (n)**, or **Largest 50 piles** when more were found and it is cut.
   Each row shows a spinner until Hub has measured the volume of that pile, from the base plane the list
   names (**Volumes above the best-fit plane**). When you choose another base plane, Hub measures the list again.
   With no pile found the panel says **No piles found. Raise the detection sensitivity to find finer ones.**

   Point at a row to emphasise its outline on the map. Click a row, or click the outline on the map, to
   choose the pile: the map zooms to it when you click the row, the list scrolls to it when you click the
   map, and the panel shows its result. Choosing a pile does not add it to the measurements. To add
   piles, click **Add** on a row, tick rows and click **Add selected (n)**, or click **Add all**. A pile that
   is added shows a check mark and is dimmed on the map: it is a measurement of the map, and a pile is
   added once. The row of an added pile shows the volume and the outline of the measurement: when you
   edit its outline or change its base plane, the row follows. When you search again later (after a reload,
   for example), a pile that you added from the list on the same elevation model shows the check mark too,
   also when you edited its outline since. If you delete the measurement, the check mark goes, the row
   gets the outline that was found back, and you can add the pile again. The
   piles you add are not saved until you click **Save Measurements**. **Clear**, a new search, another
   elevation model and closing the panel take the found piles off the map.

4. Select a **Material**. The panel shows the **Weight** and the **Est. cost** of the pile.

   <Screenshot id="analyse/stockpile--material-cost" />

5. Type a title and a description under **Identification**. Click **Save Measurements** in the
   measure toolbar to save the pile, or **Export GeoJSON** to download its outline. Hub saves the
   pile in the [measurements file of the map](/hub-guide/measure/map.mdx), with the elevation model it
   was measured on and its base plane.

</Steps>

## Work on a pile that is on the map

While **Stockpile Volume** is open, click a pile on the map, saved or not, to choose it. You can also
open the **Measurement List** of the measure toolbar and click the stockpile button of a pile: Hub opens
**Stockpile Volume** on the elevation model the pile was measured on (when it is on the map) and chooses
the pile. The panel
shows its result, its base plane, its material, its title and its description, and the line **Measured
on …** when the pile was measured on another elevation model than the one the panel is on. The base plane,
the flat elevation, the material, the title and the description in the panel are those of that pile
only. A change to the pile is an unsaved change of the map until you save it.

- **Base plane method**: Hub measures the pile again from the new base, on the elevation model it was
  measured on. With **Flat (elevation = 0)**, a **Flat elevation** field sets the elevation of the plane.
- **Material**: only the **Weight** and the **Est. cost** change.
- **Edit outline** (in the footer of the panel, while a pile is chosen): drag a corner to move it, drag the
  middle of an edge to add a corner, press `Alt` and click a corner to remove it, and press `Shift` and drag to move the
  whole pile. `Ctrl+Z` undoes a change and `Esc` or **Done editing** ends the mode. Only the chosen pile can be edited. When you stop
  moving the outline, Hub measures the pile again, also when the panel is closed and when you edit with the
  **Edit/Move Measurements** tool of the measure toolbar. If Hub cannot measure the new outline, the label of the pile says **(not recalculated)**,
  the pile keeps the figures of the previous outline, and a message says so.

## See the pile on an orthophoto

Add the orthophoto as a layer to see the pile as in a photo.

<Steps>

1. Open the DSM on the map. Click **Add layer** in the opacity control at the bottom.

2. Select the orthophoto in any folder. It goes on top of the DSM, and the map stays as one map. A view has up to 6 layers. **Remove layer** takes one off.

   <Screenshot id="analyse/stockpile--ortho-under-dsm" />

</Steps>

With two or more elevation models on the map, **Stockpile Volume** shows an **Elevation model**
selector. At first it uses the top one. Hub saves a pile with
the model you select.

An opaque orthophoto on top hides the **Raster Analysis** colours of the DSM below it. Hide it with its
eye in the layer panel, or lower its opacity. The volume does not depend on the other layers.

## Options

| Option | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|
| **Base Plane Method** | The surface under the pile you work on. See the table below. **Flat elevation** sets the plane of **Flat**. | Lowest perimeter for a drawn polygon; Best-fit plane for a detected pile |
| **Detection Sensitivity** | How easily the detection separates the pile from the ground, from 0 to 1. Higher values find lower piles, but can take in nearby ground. | 0.50 |
| **Search Radius (m)** | How far from your click Hub looks for the edge of the pile. A pile that is longer than this area is not found whole: use a radius that contains the whole pile. It is used by **Click map** only, not by **Find all piles**. | 50 |
| **Material (optional)** | The material that gives the weight and the cost. **+ Custom…** lets you type a density and a cost for this pile only. The density must be more than 0 and at most 30 t/m³ (the panel says the limit in your unit, for example **Density must be greater than 0**); with a density that is refused, **Weight** and **Est. cost** say why instead of disappearing. | none |

<Screenshot id="analyse/stockpile--base-methods" />

| Base plane method | What it is | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| **Lowest perimeter** | A flat plane at the lowest point of the outline. Fill is always 0. | A pile on uneven ground. |
| **Average perimeter** | A flat plane at the mean elevation of the outline. | A pile on level ground. |
| **Best-fit plane** | A tilted plane fitted to the outline. | A pile on a slope. |
| **Flat (elevation = 0)** | A plane at elevation 0. | A raster of heights above the ground only. |

### Results

| Result | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Cut volume** | The material above the base plane. |
| **Fill volume** | The space below the base plane. It is not shown when the method makes it 0. |
| **Net volume** | Cut minus fill. |
| **2D area** / **3D surface** | The area of the outline on the map, and of the surface of the pile. |
| **Base elevation** | The elevation of the base plane. |
| **Confidence** | For a detected pile: how sure Hub is of the outline. It is below 50 % when the outline does not reach the foot of the pile. |
| **Weight** | The larger of the cut and the net volume, times the density of the material. |
| **Est. cost** | The weight times the price of the material. |

**Clear** resets the panel, the found piles and the detection overlay. A pile that you already saved on the
map stays there, and so does a pile that you added from the list or chose on the map. A pile that you just
found with **Click map** or drew, and did not save, is removed.

## Materials, units and currency

The materials are yours. Click **Edit materials…** in the panel to open the **Stockpiles** tab of
the [preferences](/docs/hub-guide/account/preferences). There you change the density and the price
of each material, hide the ones you do not use and add your own. The prices are in the currency
of the **Units** tab. The currency is a label: Hub does not convert prices.

With imperial units, volumes are in yd³, weights in short tons and prices per short ton. The
label of the pile on the map and in the **Measurement List** follow the units too, after
**Map Settings > Measurement Units > Convert** and after you reload the page: they show yd³ and
ST/yd³. The saved values stay metric. Areas follow the same scale as the
[measurements](/hub-guide/measure/map.mdx#units). A saved pile keeps the material it was weighed
with.

## Limits & requirements

- **Stockpile Volume** shows when the map has a raster with one band. It is off while the map is split.
- Layers, opacity, order and eye are not part of the address. A user with write access saves them in a
  [saved view](/hub-guide/viewing/map-views.mdx).
- **Edit materials…** needs a signed-in account.
- Saving the pile needs write access to the dataset.
- **Click map** and **Draw** work on one pile each time. **Find all piles** lists up to 50 piles, measures
  four at a time, and you add the ones you want to the measurements.
- **Edit outline** and the choice of a pile on the map work on the piles of the measurements of the map.
