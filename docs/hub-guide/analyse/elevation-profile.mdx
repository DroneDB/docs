## When to use it

Draw a profile to see how the ground rises and falls along a line: the slope of a road, the
height of a bank, the shape of a ditch. On a thermal raster, the same tool shows the temperatures
along the line.

## Steps

<Steps>

1. Open the elevation model on the map. Click **Raster Analysis** in the toolbar at the top
   right.

   <Screenshot id="analyse/elevation-profile--panel" />

2. Click **Profile**. Click the points of the line on the map. Double-click, right-click or press
   `Enter` to finish. Press `Esc` or click **Stop** to cancel. See the
   [gestures of the map tools](/hub-guide/measure/map.mdx#keys-and-gestures).

3. The **Value Profile** chart shows the values along the line, from its start. Move the pointer
   over the chart: a dot shows the same place on the map.

   <Screenshot id="analyse/elevation-profile--chart" />

4. Click the expand button under the chart (tooltip **Show the chart larger**) to open it in a wide window. The pointer still shows its place on the map, and the dot goes when you close the window. Click **Export CSV** under the chart to download the samples.

5. Click **Redraw** to draw a new line. The eraser button removes the chart and the line.

</Steps>

## Options

| Option | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|
| **Unit** | The unit the chart shows with the values, for example `m`. | Read from the file; else `m` for an elevation model and none for other rasters |
| **Profile** / **Redraw** | Draws a new line. The current line goes. | — |
| **Export CSV** | Downloads the samples as `raster-profile.csv`. | — |

The line is also a measurement. It shows in the **Measurement List**, and **Save Measurements**
saves it with the other [measurements on the map](/hub-guide/measure/map.mdx).

## Limits & requirements

- The profile works on a raster with one band. **Raster Analysis** shows when the map has such a
  raster. With two or more, an **Elevation model** selector sets the one the profile is drawn on.
- Hub samples the line at 256 evenly spaced points.
- The distances on the chart follow the units of your [preferences](/hub-guide/account/preferences.mdx),
  as the measurements on the map: m up to 100 m, then km, or ft. The values stay in the **Unit** of
  the raster.
- The CSV always has the distances in metres (`distance_m`), with the columns `index`, `distance_m`,
  `value`, `unit`, `lon` and `lat`. It opens in Excel or any spreadsheet.
