## When to use it

Use the thermal tools to find hot and cold spots: a leak in a roof, a failed solar panel, cold
water flowing into a river. Hub paints the thermal raster with a thermal colormap, shows the
temperature range and tells you the temperature of any point you click.

## Steps

<Steps>

1. Open the thermal raster on the map. Click **Raster Analysis** in the toolbar at the top right.
   For a thermal raster, the panel is named **Thermal**.

2. Read the **Temperature Range** of the raster: the lowest and the highest temperature of the pixels that have a value. Select the **Unit**: **Celsius (°C)**,
   **Kelvin (K)** or **Fahrenheit (°F)**. The panel starts in the unit of the file (a Kelvin raster reads in K), and
   every number follows the unit you select: the range, **Inspect**, **Area**, **Profile**, the legend and the contours.

3. Select a thermal **Colormap**. Type a **Rescale** range under **Min** and **Max**, for example `10`
   and `40`, to give all the colours to the temperatures you care about. The fields start with the range of the raster;
   clear them to go back to the automatic range. A rescale keeps its temperatures when you change the unit: `10` to `40` in °C
   becomes `283.15` to `313.15` in K, and the label says **Rescale (°C)** while the unit that you typed it in is not the one shown.

4. Click **Inspect**, then click on the map. A label shows the value at that point. Press `Esc` to
   stop.

   <Screenshot id="analyse/thermal--inspect" />

5. Click **Profile** and draw a line to see the temperatures along it. See
   [Elevation profile](/hub-guide/analyse/elevation-profile.mdx); the chart is the **Thermal Profile**.

</Steps>

## Options

| Option | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|
| **Unit** | **Celsius (°C)**, **Kelvin (K)** or **Fahrenheit (°F)**. The server converts the values from the unit of the file, so a value is never shifted twice. | The unit of the file |
| **Colormap** | Ironbow, White Hot, Black Hot, Arctic, Lava, Inferno, Magma, Plasma. | Ironbow |
| **Rescale** | The lowest and the highest value of the colormap, in the **Min** and **Max** fields, in the unit shown. A link to the map keeps the unit of the rescale. | The default stretch of the raster (its mean ± 2.33 standard deviations, inside its range) |
| **Inspect** | Shows the value at the point you click. | — |
| **Original** | Shows the raster without the colormap. | — |
| **Export**, **Visible extent** | Downloads the raster as you see it, as a styled GeoTIFF picture in the coordinate system of the file (**Export styled image (RGBA GeoTIFF, in the CRS of the file)**): colours, not temperatures. **Visible extent** exports the part under the map. A task shows the progress and a **Cancel** button. See [Export a raster](/hub-guide/processing/export-raster.mdx). | — |

## How Hub finds the temperatures

Hub treats a raster as thermal when the camera is a known thermal camera (for example a DJI H20T or a FLIR),
or when the unit of its band is a temperature unit (Celsius, K or Fahrenheit) or its description says temperature or thermal. To have a
mosaic recognised, set the unit of its band (`gdal_edit.py -units Celsius`). Hub converts the raw values of a
camera to temperatures with the calibration data of the file. The panel then says what the values are:

- **A raster of temperatures** (the unit of its band is known): the values are those of the file, in its unit, and the panel says **Values in °C from the file.**
  A Kelvin raster shows 284.6 K, and 11.5 °C when you select **Celsius (°C)**. This is the case of every thermal GeoTIFF with a unit.
- **Raw counts of a sensor** (no calibration data, no unit): the panel says **No thermal calibration data: Showing raw values.** and offers no unit,
  because the values are not temperatures.

A thermal photo (a radiometric JPEG) is read the same way, from the data that its camera wrote in the file:

- **A radiometric FLIR picture**: Hub reads the five Planck constants and the settings of the measurement (emissivity, distance, reflected
  and air temperature, humidity) from the camera information record of the FLIR data in the file, and the raw thermal image from its
  raw data record. The temperatures follow from them. A file that lacks the Planck constants has no temperatures, and a raw count
  outside the calibration has no value (not 0 °C).
- **A radiometric DJI picture** (M2EA, H20T, H20N, M30T, M3T and M3TD, H30T and any model with raw thermal data): Hub knows it is thermal and reads its raw
  thermal data, but DroneDB does not have DJI's own conversion of the raw counts yet. It shows the raw counts **without a unit**, never
  as °C, and says so in a notice, until that conversion is integrated.
- **Raw data stored as a PNG image** (some FLIR files) is not decoded yet: the picture shows its colour preview, without a unit.

These pictures open in the [photo viewer](/hub-guide/viewing/photos.mdx), which shows the temperature under the pointer.
Rescan a dataset that was indexed before DroneDB 2.3 ([Rescan](/hub-guide/files/rescan.mdx)) to have its DJI pictures
recognised, and its wide and zoom pictures (not thermal) no longer marked as thermal.

A thermal mosaic that Hub does not recognise as thermal opens as a value raster. The panel is then
**Raster Analysis**, with the colormaps of other single-band rasters, and the values are those of
the file. The Hat Creek mosaics of the example open like this: their values are tenths of a degree Celsius (157 is 15.7 °C) and 0 is no data.
Type `0` in **NoData value**, and read the values as tenths of a degree. The contour dialog says **value**, not elevation, for such a raster.

## Limits & requirements

- **Raster Analysis** works on one raster at a time. With two or more rasters it can work on, a
  **Raster** selector sets the one it uses. A raster on top with full opacity hides its
  colours: hide that raster with its eye in the layer panel.
- A thermal photo (a radiometric JPEG) has no **Raster Analysis** on the map. Read its temperatures in the
  [photo viewer](/hub-guide/viewing/photos.mdx), or process the photos into a thermal orthophoto first.
- **Export** is greyed out when the server estimates the file (the pixels that have a value, 4 bytes each) as larger than its export limit; the panel says the estimate and the limit under it, and **Visible extent** exports a part. See [Export a raster](/hub-guide/processing/export-raster.mdx).
