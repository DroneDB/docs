## When to use it

A multispectral orthophoto has more bands than a screen has colours. Select which three bands
show as red, green and blue to make a false-colour image. A raster with one band, such as an
elevation model, has no colours of its own: Hub paints it with a colormap, and you choose the
colormap and the range of values.

## Steps

### Bands of a multispectral raster

<Steps>

1. Open the multispectral orthophoto on the map. Click the **Plant Health** button (a leaf) next to
   the opacity slider.

2. Keep **Formula** at **None (bands only)**. Under **Bands (R,G,B)**, select the band for each
   of **R**, **G** and **B**.

   <Screenshot id="analyse/colormaps--bands" />

3. To set the range of values, select a **Formula**, for example NDVI (see
   [Plant health](/hub-guide/analyse/plant-health.mdx)). Drag the handles of its **Histogram**:
   Hub stretches the colours over that range. With **None (bands only)** the panel has no
   histogram.

   <Screenshot id="analyse/colormaps--histogram" />

</Steps>

### Colormap of a single-band raster

<Steps>

1. Open a raster with one band on the map, for example a DSM. Click **Raster Analysis** in the
   toolbar at the top right.

2. A raster of values (an elevation model, a thermal mosaic) is painted when it opens, with
   **Viridis** (**Ironbow** for a thermal raster) over its default stretch, so it is not a black and
   white mask. Select another **Colormap**. Type a **Rescale** range under **Min** and **Max**, for
   example `0` and `50`. Both fields start with the default stretch of the raster; clear both for the
   automatic range. Hub checks the range before it applies it: both numbers must be numbers, **Min** below
   **Max**, and an error under the field names the problem. The map keeps the last valid range meanwhile,
   so a typo does not blank the layer.

   <Screenshot id="analyse/colormaps--colormap-dropdown" />

3. Click **Reset** to put the colormap, the **Rescale** range, the **Unit** and the **NoData value** back
   to the defaults of the raster.

</Steps>

## Options

| Option | Panel | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bands (R,G,B)** | Plant Health | The band that shows as red, green and blue. It shows for rasters with more than three bands. | The bands Hub matched to the camera |
| **Histogram** | Plant Health | The values of the first band, with two handles that set the range. Far outliers are left out, and a note names them; see [Plant health](/hub-guide/analyse/plant-health.mdx). | — |
| **Colormap** | Plant Health (with a formula), Raster Analysis | The colours of the values. | Red-Yellow-Green (index), Viridis (raster), Ironbow (thermal) |
| **Rescale** | Both | The lowest and the highest value of the colormap, in the **Min** and **Max** fields. Clear both for the automatic range. | The default stretch of the raster: its mean ± 2.33 standard deviations, kept inside its range (from the server; an 8-bit raster of several bands keeps its own colours) |
| **Unit** | Raster Analysis | The unit that the panel shows with the values, for example `m`. | Read from the file; else `m` for an elevation model and none for other rasters |
| **Reset** | Both | Puts the colormap, the **Rescale** range, the **Unit** and the **NoData value** back to their defaults. | — |
| **Original** | Both | Shows the raster without the styling. Click **Hide Orig.** to go back. | — |
| **Export** | Both | Downloads the raster as you see it, as a GeoTIFF. | — |

### Colormaps

| Raster | Colormaps |
|---|---|
| Vegetation index | Red-Yellow-Green, Contrast NDVI, Spectral, Viridis, Plasma, Inferno, Magma, Grayscale, Ironbow, Rainbow, Blue-Green, White Hot, Black Hot, Arctic, Lava |
| Single band (elevation, values) | Viridis, Terrain, Magma, Plasma, Inferno, Greys |
| Thermal | Ironbow, White Hot, Black Hot, Arctic, Lava, Inferno, Magma, Plasma |

## Limits & requirements

- **Raster Analysis** shows in the toolbar when the map shows a raster with one data band, or a
  thermal image. With two or more such rasters, an **Elevation model** selector sets the one it
  works on. A raster on top with full opacity hides the colormap of the one below: hide it with
  its eye in the layer panel, or lower its opacity.
- **Export** is greyed out when the server estimates the file (the pixels that have a value, 4 bytes each) as larger than its export limit; its tooltip gives the limit.
- The settings apply to what you see. They do not change the file.
- The style stays on the map when you close **Raster Analysis** or **Plant Health**, and a closed panel
  changes nothing. Only an edit or **Reset** changes it. Opening **Stockpile Volume** does not
  recolour the raster, and changing the colormap or the range swaps the tiles in place, so the
  map does not go blank. A layer that a [saved view](/hub-guide/viewing/map-views.mdx) records keeps the
  style of the view.
- A raster with more than 8 bits per sample, such as a 16-bit multispectral orthophoto, opens stretched over the range of its values (about its mean ± 2.33 standard deviations), not over the whole 16-bit range, so it is not almost black.
