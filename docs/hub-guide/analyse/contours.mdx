## When to use it

Contour lines show the shape of the ground on a flat map: each line joins the points at the same
elevation. Use them to read the slope of a site, to plan drainage, or to give the terrain to
someone who works in a drawing program.

## Steps

<Steps>

1. Open the elevation model on the map. Click **Raster Analysis** in the toolbar at the top
   right.

2. Click **Contours**. The **Generate Contour Lines** dialog opens.

   <Screenshot id="analyse/contours--dialog" />

3. Select the **Spacing**: a **Fixed interval** between the lines, or a **Target count** of
   lines. Set the other options if you need them. Click **Generate**.

4. The dialog stays open and shows the task of the contours with its progress. Click **Cancel**
   in the dialog to stop it. A request of more than 50 levels asks first: see
   [Limits & requirements](#limits--requirements).

5. When the task ends the dialog closes and the lines show on the map, coloured from low (blue)
   to high (white).

   <Screenshot id="analyse/contours--result" />

6. Click **Save Measurements** in the measure toolbar to keep the lines in the dataset, or
   **Export Measurements** to download them as GeoJSON.

</Steps>

## Options

| Option | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixed interval** / **Interval** | The difference in elevation between two lines, in the units of the raster. | 1 |
| **Target count** / **Number of levels** | The number of lines, from 2 to 500. Hub shows the spacing this gives. | 20 |
| **Base elevation** | The elevation that the lines count from. With an interval of 5 and a base of 2, the lines are at 2, 7, 12 … | 0 |
| **Min elevation** / **Max elevation** | Only lines between these elevations. Empty: the range of the raster. | Empty |
| **Simplification tolerance** | Makes the lines simpler, in the units of the coordinate system of the raster. 0 disables it. | 0 |

When you open the dialog again, it shows the options you used last.

## Limits & requirements

- **Raster Analysis** shows when the map has a raster with one band. With two or more elevation
  models, the **Elevation model** selector sets the one the contours are drawn on. The **Contours**
  button is not there for thermal rasters.
- Contours are measurements: they are saved, listed and exported with the other
  [measurements on the map](/hub-guide/measure/map.mdx). Saving needs write access to the dataset.
- **Generate** again replaces the contours that you did not save.
- Contours run as a task, like an export, so a large raster no longer blocks the page. It can
  take minutes on a very large raster, and **Cancel** in the dialog stops it; the dialog cannot be
  closed while the task runs. The task is listed as **Contour lines** in the
  [Tasks tab](/hub-guide/processing/tasks.mdx) with its log. A signed-in reader can cancel their own
  contours: the task needs read access only.
- With more than 50 levels (the **Number of levels**, or the range of the raster divided by the
  **Interval**) the dialog shows **About N contour levels** and **Generate** asks **Generate many
  contour lines?** before it starts. Click **Change settings** to go back, or **Generate** to run it.
  A larger interval, or **Min elevation** and **Max elevation**, makes it faster.
- A result has at most 100,000 lines. When the raster has more, Hub says **Contours truncated**:
  the lines are truncated at 100,000 lines and the rest of the raster has none. Use a larger
  interval or limit the elevation range, and generate again. A server older than DroneDB 2.3 cuts
  the result at 100,000 lines without saying it.
