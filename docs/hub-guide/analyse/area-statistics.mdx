## When to use it

Use the statistics of a raster to know its values before you work with it: the lowest and the
highest elevation of a site, the temperature range of a thermal mosaic, the value at one point, or
the mean height of one part of a site.

## Steps

<Steps>

1. Open the raster on the map. Click **Raster Analysis** in the toolbar at the top right. The panels of the analysis are wider on a large screen, with larger text.

2. Read the **Value Range** (the **Temperature Range** for a thermal raster): the lowest and the
   highest value of the raster, counted over the pixels that have a value, in the unit of the raster.

3. Click **Inspect**, then click on the map. A label shows the value of the pixel under the click.
   The panel keeps the last value as **Spot Value** (**Spot Temperature** for a thermal raster), with
   the latitude and longitude of the point that you clicked. Press `Esc` to stop.

   <Screenshot id="analyse/area-statistics--value-range" />

4. Click **Area**. Click one corner of a rectangle on the map, then the opposite corner (or press `Space` at the crosshair in the centre of the map for each corner). The panel
   shows the **Area Statistics** of the pixels inside it. Press `Esc` or click **Stop** to cancel the
   drawing.

5. To remove the rectangle and its statistics, click the eraser next to **Area Statistics**. A new
   rectangle replaces the old one.

6. Optional: if the background of the raster is not marked as no data in the file, for example a
   thermal mosaic with a border of zeros, type its value under **NoData value**. Hub reads the raster
   again with it: the range, **Inspect**, **Area**, the profile and the colours on the map all leave
   those pixels out. The field shows the value of the file as its hint, for example `-9999 (of the
   file)`. Click **Reset** to go back to the value of the file.

7. For a raster with two or more bands, open [Plant health](/hub-guide/analyse/plant-health.mdx). Its
   **Statistics** show the minimum, the maximum, the mean and the standard deviation of the first
   band, and its **Histogram** shows how the values spread.

</Steps>

## Options

| Statistic | Where | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Value Range** / **Temperature Range** | Raster Analysis | The lowest and the highest value of the whole raster: exact, over the pixels that have a value, not an estimate from a reduced copy. |
| **Inspect** | Raster Analysis | The value of the pixel you click, with the latitude and longitude of the point that you clicked. |
| **NoData value** | Raster Analysis | The value that stands for "no data" and replaces the one of the file, for the colours on the map and for every number of the panel. Empty: the value of the file. |
| **Area** | Raster Analysis | **Min**, **Max**, **Mean**, **Median** and **Std dev** of the pixels in the rectangle, in the unit of the raster, the number of **Pixels** counted and, if there are some, the pixels with **No data**. |
| **Statistics** | Plant Health | Min, Max, Mean and StdDev of the first band of the whole raster. |
| **Histogram** | Plant Health | How many pixels have each value. |

## Limits & requirements

- **Raster Analysis** shows when the map has a raster with one band, or a thermal raster. With
  two or more, an **Elevation model** selector sets the one it works on. It is not available
  while the map is split.
- One map tool works at a time: **Area**, **Inspect** or **Profile**. A new one stops the other.
  The measure toolbar waits while you draw the rectangle.
- Every value shows with its unit in one format: a temperature with one decimal and a symbol (`29.1 °C`,
  `K`, `°F`), an elevation in `m` or `ft` with two decimals, and any other value with up to three. The
  range, **Spot Value**, **Area Statistics**, the **Inspect** label and the profile chart use it.
- The values are those of the source file, in the unit of the band: the scale and the offset of the
  band are applied (a band stored in tenths of a degree shows `29.1`, not `291`), and the pixels
  that the file masks or marks as no data are left out.
- Pixels with the nodata value of the band do not count in **Area Statistics**. A rectangle outside
  the raster, or with only such pixels, shows **No statistics for this area: it may hold only pixels
  without data.**
- **Inspect** and **Area** work in any coordinate system, Web Mercator or UTM, with the same
  accuracy: Hub sends the point or the corners as longitude and latitude, and the server finds the
  pixels of the file under them, with the conversion that the elevation profile and the stockpile
  tools also use. **Area** counts the pixels whose centre is inside the rectangle.
- If the no-data value of the file cannot be held by its data type (65536 in a 16-bit band), no pixel
  is treated as no data and the panel says so: type the value of the background under **NoData
  value**.
- Another raster, or closing the panel, removes the rectangle.

<ForDevelopers api="/docs/developers/api/workflows">

`GET /orgs/{org}/ds/{ds}/raster-area-stats?path=…&bbox=minLon,minLat,maxLon,maxLat` returns `min`,
`max`, `mean`, `stddev`, `median` and `pixelCount` of the pixels of the file under the window
(WGS 84). The **Area** tool sends the corners of the rectangle you draw as `bbox`, and **Inspect**
sends `raster-point-value?path=…&lon=…&lat=…`. Add `nodata=` for the **NoData value**. The
[Processing platform API](/developers/processing-api.mdx#raster-values) lists the parameters.

</ForDevelopers>
