## When to use it

Use the statistics of a raster to know its values before you work with it: the lowest and the
highest elevation of a site, the temperature range of a thermal mosaic, the value at one point.

## Steps

<Steps>

1. Open the raster on the map. Click **Raster Analysis** in the toolbar at the top right.

2. Read the **Value Range** (the **Temperature Range** for a thermal raster): the lowest and the
   highest value of the raster.

3. Click **Inspect**, then click on the map. A label shows the value at that point. Press `Esc` to
   stop.

4. For a raster with two or more bands, open [Plant health](/hub-guide/analyse/plant-health.mdx). Its
   **Statistics** show the minimum, the maximum, the mean and the standard deviation of the first
   band, and its **Histogram** shows how the values spread.

</Steps>

## Options

| Statistic | Where | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Value Range** / **Temperature Range** | Raster Analysis | The lowest and the highest value of the whole raster. |
| **Inspect** | Raster Analysis | The value of the pixel you click. |
| **Statistics** | Plant Health | Min, Max, Mean and StdDev of the first band of the whole raster. |
| **Histogram** | Plant Health | How many pixels have each value. |

### Statistics of a rectangle

The Hub server computes the statistics of a rectangle of pixels of any single-band raster: Min,
Max, Mean, StdDev, Median and the number of pixels. The Hub pages do not have a tool to draw the
rectangle: use the API (see **For developers** below).

## Limits & requirements

- **Raster Analysis** shows when the map has exactly one raster with one band, or one thermal
  raster.
- Pixels with the nodata value of the band do not count in the statistics of a rectangle.

<ForDevelopers api="/docs/developers/api/workflows">

`GET /orgs/{org}/ds/{ds}/raster-area-stats?path=…&x0=…&y0=…&x1=…&y1=…` returns `min`, `max`,
`mean`, `stddev`, `median` and `pixelCount` of the rectangle between the pixels (`x0`, `y0`) and
(`x1`, `y1`) of the raster.

</ForDevelopers>
