## When to use it

Open a photo to look at it at full size, zoom into a detail and read its information. For a **radiometric thermal photo** (an R-JPEG of a FLIR camera or a DJI thermal drone) the viewer also gives the temperature of any pixel.

## Steps

<Steps>

1. In the file list, double-click a photo. It opens over the page. When the folder has more photos, the arrows beside the photo (or the `Left` and `Right` keys) go to the previous and the next one.

2. Zoom with the wheel, a pinch, a double-click or the **Zoom in** and **Zoom out** buttons. **Fit to screen** (or `0`) goes back to the whole photo. A zoomed photo is dragged, and the arrow keys pan it.

3. Click **Information** (or press `I`) to read the file information beside the photo. **Open in new tab** opens the file, and **Close** (or `Escape`) closes the viewer.

4. For a thermal photo with temperatures, a bar under the title has the thermal controls. Select the **Unit**: **Celsius (°C)**, **Kelvin (K)** or **Fahrenheit (°F)**. The viewer starts in °C, and every number follows the unit.

5. Select a **Palette**: **As shot** or a thermal colormap such as Ironbow, White Hot or Lava. The photo is drawn again with it, and a legend at the top left shows the colours over the range.

6. Read the **Range** of the photo (its lowest and highest temperature), and move the pointer over the photo: the bar shows the temperature under it and the pixel of the thermal image, for example **21.4 °C at 120, 96**. A pixel with no temperature says **No value**.

7. The **Information** panel has a **Thermal** group: the sensor, the range and the settings that the file has (emissivity, distance, reflected and air temperature, humidity).

</Steps>

## Options

| Option | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|
| **Unit** | Shows the range and the temperatures in °C, K or °F. The server converts them, so a value is never shifted twice. | Celsius (°C) |
| **Palette** | **As shot** or a thermal colormap, with a legend. | As shot |

## Limits & requirements

- A photo that is thermal but has **no temperatures** shows as the camera made it, with a notice instead of the controls: **No thermal calibration data: showing the picture as shot, without temperatures.** The file has no raw thermal image that can be read, or no calibration to turn it into temperatures.
- A **DJI** thermal photo says that its temperatures need DJI's own conversion of the raw sensor values, **which is not available yet**. The viewer does not show raw counts as degrees. FLIR photos with the Planck constants in the file have temperatures.
- A FLIR photo whose raw thermal data is stored as a PNG image is not decoded yet: it shows as shot.
- The temperature is that of a pixel of the thermal image, which is smaller than the photo for many cameras; the pointer is mapped to it at any zoom.
- After a DroneDB upgrade, [rescan the dataset](/hub-guide/files/rescan.mdx) so that DJI thermal photos are marked as thermal and the wide and zoom photos of DJI thermal drones are not.
