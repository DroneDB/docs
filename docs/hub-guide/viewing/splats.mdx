## When to use it

A Gaussian splat is a photo-real 3D scene made from photos or video. Use the splat viewer to walk through it and show it from saved camera views. Hub streams only the detail that the view needs, so a large scene opens quickly.

## Steps

<Steps>

1. In the file list, double-click a splat, or right-click it and choose **Open Gaussian Splat**. The viewer opens in a new browser tab.

   <Screenshot id="viewing/splats--overview" />

2. Choose a navigation mode with the buttons at the right edge:
   - **Fly navigation**: drag to look around. Move with `W` `A` `S` `D` or the arrow keys, `Q` down and `E` up. Hold `Shift` to go faster. Right-drag pans, the wheel zooms.
   - **Earth navigation**: drag to pan the ground, right-drag to orbit around the point under the pointer, and use the wheel to zoom to the pointer.

3. Use the camera rail at the left edge. Click a number, or press `1` to `9`, to fly to a saved camera view. Press `-` or `+` for the previous or next view. Click **Start orbit** to turn slowly around the scene. Click **Start cycling**, or press `P`, to tour the saved views. Any move of yours stops the orbit or the tour.

4. Click **Manage camera views** (the camera button at the right edge) to list the views. Users with write access can add the current view, replace, rename, reorder and remove views, then click **Save**.

   <Screenshot id="viewing/splats--camera-views" />

5. Click **View settings** (the gear) when the scene is upside down, on its side, or slow. See the options below.

   <Screenshot id="viewing/splats--settings" />

6. Click **About this splat** (the (i) button) to see the number of splats, the size, the georeference, the estimated up and the quality, and the [downloads](./splat-downloads.mdx).

   <Screenshot id="viewing/splats--about" />

</Steps>

## Options

| Option | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|
| **Up axis** | Which axis of the scene points up. **Auto** follows the up that Hub estimated when it built the splat. The other choices are the six axes, for example **−Y (upside down)** or **+Z (Z-up scene)** | Auto when Hub has a reliable estimate, else −Y |
| **Quality** | **Auto**, **Low**, **Medium** or **High**: how much colour detail, level of detail and sharpness the viewer draws. **Auto** starts at High and lowers the detail when the view runs under 30 frames per second | Auto |
| **Point size** | Makes the splats smaller or larger, from 0.25x to 3x | 1x |
| **Ground plane calibration** | **Roll** and **Pitch** of up to 45°, to make walls and doors vertical. **Reset** sets both to 0 | 0° |

The navigation mode and the quality are [preferences](../account/preferences.mdx) (**Viewers** tab). Hub remembers the up axis, the point size and the calibration of each splat in this browser. When a user with write access saves the views, the up axis and the calibration are saved too, and everyone sees the scene the same way.

## Share a view

The saved camera views belong to the splat, so everyone who opens it gets them. To show a place in the scene to someone, save it as a camera view, then [share the link](../sharing/links-embeds.mdx) of the splat and tell them its number.

## Limits & requirements

- The splat opens after its build is done. Until then the viewer says that it is not built yet. See [Builds](../processing/builds.mdx).
- The viewer needs WebGL 2.
- Only users with write access can save camera views. Hub keeps them in a file next to the splat (`train_cameras.json` for `train.ply`).
